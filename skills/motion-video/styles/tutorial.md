# Style: tutorial

*Also known as: screencast, tutorial video, UI walkthrough, how-to video.*

A help-centre clip: one task, done on the real product by a cursor, in numbered steps a viewer can pause on.

- **Use for:** onboarding, help articles, a "how to" under a feature launch, a support reply.
- **Length:** 30-90 s. **Voice:** recommended (voiceover-sync); captions always (no voice: the timeline's `captions`, drawn and exported from one list, each step's caption held for its step). **Formats:** 16:9.
- **Scenes:** hook (the finished result, 2 s) → chapter-title (step N) → demo (the step, cursor-driven) × N → proof (the result again) → end card with the help link.
- **Music:** the kit track (121 BPM) low in the mix unless a slower one is added to kit.py's list, ducked 15 dB under the voice.

## Pacing
Slower than a launch film: each click lands, holds 1.5 beats (the longest any hold may be), then the next. One action per step. Step numbers stay on screen.

## Canvas, type, UI
The real product, full frame, via motion-capture states (one capture per step). Step labels on white cards, 32 px (default).

## Motion grammar
- The camera follows the cursor with log-space zoom; the cursor scales with the zoom.
- The field or button being used gets a soft accent ring that springs in, never a glow.

## Camera
Zoom to where the work happens, pull back between steps so the viewer keeps their bearings.

## Colour meaning
Accent = the thing to click next.

## Banned
Skipping a step, a cursor that teleports, typing faster than a person reads, any UI not captured from the product.
