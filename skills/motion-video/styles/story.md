# Style: story film

*Also known as: narrative animation, storytelling animation, brand story video.*

Showreel energy, but it tells a story with a start, a turn and an end: the history of a problem, how a team works before and after, a founder's why.

- **Use for:** a brand film, an about page, a keynote opener, a history-of-the-category piece.
- **Length:** 45-150 s. **Voice:** optional (see scenes/voiceover-sync.md). **Formats:** 16:9 or 9:16, chosen at intake.
- **Scenes:** hook (the most striking image, first 2 s) → 3-5 chapters (chapter-title, then scenes) → payoff → end card. A new visual payoff every 3-5 seconds.
- **Music:** from the kit, measured. Chapter turns land on downbeats; the payoff lands on the drop.

## Product rule
Real product UI still only comes from real screenshots. Drawn story elements (a timeline, a map, a whiteboard, a character) are allowed because they are not the product: say so in the shotlist, mark each drawn element "illustration".

## Character
If there is a character, it is a role ("a support lead"), not an invented person. Write a one-page character sheet in ANIMATION_GUIDE.md first: proportions, palette, 3 expressions. Every chapter agent draws from that sheet.

## Canvas, type
From the brand, one display face and one UI face, one accent. Default: the editorial card.

## Motion grammar
- Every chapter has its own technique (drawn line, kinetic type, map, UI morph) but the same springs and colour meaning.
- The last frame of each chapter sets up the first frame of the next: a shape carries over.

## Build
Up to about 90 s, chapters are sections of one timeline. Longer films build chapters as separate clips in parallel, all reading ANIMATION_GUIDE.md, joined on one timeline.

## Banned
Stock-style generic characters, invented quotes or dates, anything from a video model presented as the product, glows, crossfades.
